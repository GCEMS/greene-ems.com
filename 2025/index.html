From the Chief

2025 was a busy year. Our six paramedic units were dispatched to 4,125 calls, rode to the hospital with 2,189 patients, and had a paramedic on scene in under nine minutes on average, in the valley and on the mountaintop.

This page is the short version of what that looked like. Thank you to the ambulance crews, fire departments, dispatchers, and hospitals we work with every day, and to the towns and the county for backing us.

Steve Near, Chief of Operations

Why a paramedic

Treatment starts at the scene.

Strokes, heart attacks, asthma attacks, overdoses: the first treatment happens where the patient is. Our units carry the monitors, ventilators, and medications to start it, and a paramedic to use them.

What the state found

The model New York now recommends

In March 2026 the New York State Rural Ambulance Services Task Force reported to the Legislature. Of its 38 proposals, number 8 is tiered response: a local ambulance plus a paramedic fly car, with the closest appropriate unit sent to every call. Greene County has run that system since 2000.

Read the task force report

"Tiered response models consisting of BLS transporting units and ALS non transporting EMS vehicles may greatly increase response capabilities and create more resilient response systems by preserving ALS level care for when it is needed."

New York State Report on Rural Ambulance Services, 2026, page 24
