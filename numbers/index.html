2022 to 2025

What changed, year by year

2022

A new Chief of Operations

In February, Chief Brucato left for the New York State Division of EMS, where he is now Chief of Emergency Preparedness, and Steve Near became Chief of Operations. Breathing support that year was a bag-valve mask, a CPAP mask, and a basic pneumatic ventilator. Intubations were done with KingVision video laryngoscopes.

2023

The first ventilators

In April, Columbia Memorial Health donated LTV 1200 transport ventilators and every provider trained on them. In June, three went into service in Medics 3, 5, and 9.

2024

Hamilton T1s and BiPAP

In March, a cardiac arrest patient was kept on the ventilator through the transport and went home with no deficit. In June, three Hamilton T1s went into service and the LTVs moved to the other three cars, so every car carried one. The T1 added BiPAP. Medic 7 moved to the Freehold Fire Department, and video laryngoscopes changed to UESCOPE.

2025

ALS Provider of the Year

The Regional Emergency Medical Organization named our Friday day shift crew ALS Provider of the Year. On January 1, three more Hamilton T1s went into service and the LTVs were retired, so all six units carry the same equipment.

How it works

A paramedic in a car, an ambulance for the ride

Greene County Paramedics does not run ambulances. A paramedic drives to the call in a Tahoe and meets the town's ambulance there. Greene County has worked this way since 2000.

At the sceneThe paramedic evaluates the patient and starts treatment with the monitor, medications, and airway equipment in the truck.
On the way inWhen a patient needs a paramedic for the ride, the paramedic rides in the ambulance to the hospital.
Back in serviceWhen a patient is stable after being evaluated, the ambulance crew makes the trip and the paramedic stays in the area for the next call.
